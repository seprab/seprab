title: Engine support goes to GDC
description: >-
  What happens when you put a support engineer in the room at GDC — and what
  studios ask in person that they never write in tickets.
pubDate: 2025-03-28

---

This year I represented my team at GDC in San Francisco, sitting in on
customer meetings alongside account and partner teams — hundreds of meetings
across the week, with me as the engineer in the room. Support work is usually
asynchronous: a ticket arrives, you investigate, you write back. GDC inverts
all of it. A technical director describes a problem in two sentences, you ask
the third question, and suddenly you're sketching memory layouts on a notepad
between sessions. The bandwidth of a face-to-face conversation still beats any
ticket thread I've ever been part of.

What struck me most is *what* people ask in person. Tickets are precise and
symptom-shaped: a crash, a regression, a build error. In person, studios ask
strategy-shaped questions — "should we be worried about our load times at this
scale?", "how do teams like ours structure their content pipeline?" — the
questions that never get filed because no single ticket contains them. My
takeaway for anyone in a support role: get in front of your users when you
can. A week of conversations recalibrated my sense of what actually hurts
studios day-to-day, and half of my investigation backlog since then traces
back to something someone said in a hallway.
